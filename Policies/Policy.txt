Konfer policy reference
=======================

Everything a policy may put in the descriptor it hands konfer:RegisterPolicy(), what each piece has to do, when the platform
calls it and with what, and everything the platform puts on the policy in return. Sections 2 to 10 are the contract as the
code implements it. Section 11 is what the platform does not yet do that a policy will need, section 12 is where the comments
in the code no longer say what the code does, and section 13 lists bugs found along the way.

This describes the code. Policy.lua documents the descriptor inline and is the contract; this says in more detail when each
hook is called and from where. Data.txt says which home every piece of state belongs in and why. File references are to
Konfer unless they name Kore.


1.  The life of a policy
------------------------

Loading
  A policy is Policies/<P>/<P>.lua, listed in Konfer.toc after every platform file and before Init.lua, with the events only
  it understands in Policies/<P>/Comms.lua straight after. <P>.lua builds a descriptor table and ends with
  konfer:RegisterPolicy(descriptor). Comms.lua reaches the registered policy as konfer.policies.<handle> and adds to its
  handler tables (section 7).

Registration  (Policy.lua RegisterPolicy)
  Validates the identity fields, normalises the art, fills in defaults, gives the policy its own copies of the standard
  handler tables and whisper commands, registers it with KoreComms as an endpoint of its own, copies the platform methods onto
  it (section 8) and stores it in konfer.policies. Every policy registers, switched off or not, so that every config's policy
  can be resolved. One that registers after Init.lua has loaded is external (see order in section 2). One that registers
  after the mod has initialised is given at once what startup gives the others: the config it had in hand, its panels when
  they are being built eagerly, its slash command, and its card in the foyer and the settings window.

Startup  (Konfer.lua OnLateInit)
  In this order. MakeAliases. UpdateDatabaseVersion. MakeAllConfigs, which puts the methods and the settings proxy back on
  every config (a metatable is not saved). Each policy is pointed at the config it had in hand at logout,
  konfer:PolicyDB(pol).defconfig, through RestorePolicyConfig, or at nothing. The active policy is frdb.activepolicy, else
  konfer.DEFAULT_POLICY ("sk"), else the first policy still switched on, and its config is made current with cfg:SetDefault.
  Then InitialiseUI (Init.lua).

Initialisation  (Init.lua InitialiseUI)
  There is one window, konfer.mainwin, and it is the mod's. InitialiseMainWindow builds it once, with every page, and the
  pages build the platform's half of themselves. A policy's own panels go into slots those pages keep for them, and are built
  by konfer:BuildPolicyUI, once per policy: BuildListsPolicyUI, BuildLootPolicyUI, BuildUsersPolicyUI, BuildConfigPolicyUI.
  When that happens is konfer.EAGER_POLICY_UI. Lazily, the first time a policy becomes the active one, is what ships; in
  development every policy is built at startup, so that a fault in any Construct shows at login. A policy switched off in the
  settings window builds nothing. Then EnterPolicy for the active policy, and the foyer, the settings window, the user editor,
  the policy slash commands and the window stack.

The active policy
  Exactly one policy is konfer.apol. It is the one the window shows and the one the loot engine runs. Every route to a switch
  goes through konfer:ActivatePolicy(pol): the foyer's Activate, which asks nothing first, double-clicking a card or typing
  another policy's command, which ask first (konfer:AskSwitchPolicy), and startup. It refuses outright while loot is being
  handed out (konfer:LootInProgress). Otherwise it calls LeavePolicy on the outgoing policy (OnActivate(false), then its
  panels hidden), sets apol and frdb.activepolicy, calls EnterPolicy on the incoming one (panels built if they never were and
  shown, the window given its name, accent and icon by SetWindowPolicy, then OnActivate(true)), empties konfer.raid, and
  refreshes. cfg:SetDefault only makes a config its policy's current one, with one exception: while the active policy has
  no config at all, a policy that gains or picks one becomes the active one.

A policy is never consulted about being active, and never assigns apol, currentid or cfg itself.

A config brought up to date
  When another admin brings one of a policy's configs up to date, the policy is told through ConfigSynced(cfgid, how) on its
  descriptor, HOW being "full" for a full sync (FSYNC), "events" for the events it was missing (MSYNC) and "recovered" for its
  owner's recovery of it. It is called whether or not the policy is the active one, once the platform has done its own part,
  which redraws the window only for the active policy, so it is for what a policy shows or keeps of its own. EP/GP redraws
  its Journal tab and its Lists page panel when they show that config.


2.  Identity
------------

The first five are required, must be non-empty strings, and are checked at registration, which raises a Lua error on a bad
one. name, nickname and shortnick must also be unique across every registered policy, and so must the handle.

  handle      Short string naming the policy. It may not be konfer.CMD_MOD ("*"), which is the mod's name on the wire, nor
              one of konfer.FRDB_RESERVED, the names frdb keeps for the mod. It is cfg.policy, the key in konfer.policies,
              frdb[handle] (the policy's own saved tables, see konfer:PolicyDB), the key in frdb.policyorder,
              frdb.policydisabled, the policy's endpoint on the wire (section 7), and the slash command's SlashCmdList name
              KONFER_POLICY_<HANDLE>. pol.prefix is derived from it as handle .. "_" and every frame name the policy owns
              starts with pol.prefix.

  name        Localised display name. The foyer card's text when the policy has no TitleCard, the window's page titles while
              it is the active policy (pol:WindowTitle), the title of its panels on the Admin tab, the item editor and the
              Lists page's Config tab, the settings window's list, and the tie break when two policies share an order.

  nickname    "Konfer SK". Never shown. Recognised by the chat filter that hides the mod's own whispers.

  shortnick   "KSK". What pol:err(), pol:info() and pol:warn() sign this player's own chat lines with. What goes to the raid,
              the guild or another player is signed with the mod's name instead.

  desc        Localised description of the policy, how it distributes and what it offers, as many lines as it takes. The
              platform draws it at the top of the foyer's Overview whenever the policy's card is selected.

The rest are optional.

  command     Slash command without the slash, "ksk", checked at registration against every policy and every slash command
              the game already knows. Typed bare it calls konfer:OpenPolicy(handle), which asks to switch first when another
              policy is active. With arguments it runs konfer:OnPolicyCommand(input, pol), the words every policy has
              (pcmdtab) and the policy's own (Commands), asking to switch first unless konfer:CommandNeedsPolicy says the
              word needs no policy (createconfig and help). Refused with POLICY_SWITCHED_OFF while the policy is switched
              off. A policy with none can only be reached from the foyer.

  accent      { r, g, b }, each a number, checked at registration. The window's accent while the policy is active (portrait
              frame, title plaque and its gem). Fully saturated, since the kit multiplies by it. Without one the window wears
              the kit gold.

  order       Number, where the policy sorts, lowest first, ties by name. An external policy has
              konfer.POLICY_EXTERNAL_BASE (100) added. frdb.policyorder, set in the settings window, overrides it. The five
              in the box also use it as their cell in the art strips (section 3), which is convention and not enforced.

  raid        Booleans for where the policy is usable. Only a nil is defaulted, to raid = true, party = true, bg = false.
  party       konfer:IsUsableHere(pol) answers from them, battleground first, then raid, then party, and solo is always
  bg          usable. The loot engine runs only when konfer:AmIML() and IsUsableHere() for the active policy. raid and party
              are also the policy's reach on the wire.

  costing     True for a policy whose awards carry a price the master looter may discount, every win then being put to
              them in the costing box (section 5). Only a nil is defaulted, to false. EP/GP and DKP say true.

  channel     Where every config of the policy talks, CHANNEL_GUILD or CHANNEL_OTHER, whatever the config holds (cfg:Channel).
              A new config is made with it and the Admin tab's Sync via shows it greyed. PUG says CHANNEL_OTHER.

  onelist     True for a policy whose every config runs on one list of its own, made with the config and never shown, and
              named for the policy. Everybody the config holds is on it, every character standing alone, and it is the
              config's default list, so every item is given out on it. cfg:MakeOneList makes it and cfg:JoinOneList puts
              each user added here on it, after the MKUSR. A copy is made with its own and the copy dialog offers none of
              the source's lists. Such a policy hides the Lists page. PUG says true.


3.  Art and the foyer
---------------------

Art is a texture path, which means the whole texture, or { texture = path, coords = { left, right, top, bottom } }.
Registration turns a path into the table and gives a table without coords { 0, 1, 0, 1 }. Anything else, or a table with no
texture string, is a registration error. The policies in the box take theirs from Konfer's strips with
konfer:PolicyArt(strip, order), strip being "PolicyLogos", "PolicyIcons" or "PolicyCommands".

  TitleCard   The foyer card, twice as wide as it is tall with the bottom eighth a band, drawn by konfer:CreateTitleCard. May
              instead be a function (pol, button, width, height), which draws the card into BUTTON. It is called once per
              card button, through pcall, and never again when that button is pointed at another policy. Without either the
              card is dark with the policy's name on it.

  Icon        The square behind the portrait frame in the window's top left corner while the policy is active, put there
              by konfer:SetWindowPolicy. Without one the corner is dark.

  CommandPlate  The plaque naming the command, four times as wide as it is tall, drawn at the right hand end of the card's
              band. Give one only while command is what the picture says.

  beta        True for a policy still in beta, whose card in the foyer wears Kore's Beta stamp over its artwork
              (Foyer.lua). Loot Council says true.

  PopulateBannerInfo(cfgid) -> height
              Draws what the foyer's Overview says about the config selected in the Configurations panel, under the
              policy's desc, which the platform draws at the top whatever the policy, in place of the platform's own. That
              is a box for each of the config's lists in the Lists page's order, saying how many are on it and showing the
              whole of a short list, and of a longer one its top three, this character and its bottom three, each with
              their place in the policy's Order and what ValueText says. A policy whose lists say little gives this
              instead. Called through pcall whenever a card or a config is selected and on every foyer refresh, with the
              selected config's id or nil when none is. The frame to draw on is self.foyerinfo, as wide as the panel and
              set under the description, that the platform creates the first time this policy's card is selected and
              hides whenever another card is. Answer how tall what was drawn is, nothing for nothing. Build widgets on
              first call and only update them after, since this is called often. PUG's and LC's draw nothing, so their
              description stands alone.


4.  The window's pages
----------------------

There is one window and it shows the active policy. Its pages are the mod's, from the tree in Init.lua: Lists (tabs Members
and Config), Raid (tab Raiders), Loot (tabs Assign loot, Item editor and History), Users, Sync, Config (tabs Loot and Admin)
and Options, with the config selector in the topbar. The Raid page is everybody in the group, up while the player is in one
and gone otherwise. A policy contributes panels within those pages, through the hooks below, and tabs of its own on the
Lists page (ListsTabs), the Raid page (RaidTabs) and the Config page (ConfigTabs), there while it is the active policy and
gone when it is not.

A policy may also hide the platform pages it has no use for (hidepages), and add pages of its own (Pages), after the
platform's or wherever its order puts them, each redrawn by its Refresh whenever the member lists are, both only while it is
the active policy. A page of its own may have tabs, each with a Construct and a Refresh of its own, their strip along the
page's top bar from the left. The window opens on whichever page is first. PUG hides Lists, so its window opens on the Raid
page, which it keeps up out of a group, its Reserves tab, tonight's reserves from softres.it, being imported and cleared
there by an admin and seen by everybody holding the config.

Every hook here is optional, and a missing one is simply not called. Each is called through konfer:PolicyHook, or through
cfg:PolicyCall for one that takes a config, so a hook that raises is reported once, under the policy's name and the hook's,
and the page carries on without it. CLIENT is always a plain content frame belonging to the policy, already sized, and the
policy lays out everything inside it. Frame names inside it start with pol.prefix. A policy's frames hang off the policy
(section 9) and are hidden whenever another policy is active.

Lists page, Members tab
  ListsPage   A table. With one, the policy gets a panel under Announce on the Members tab. Without one there is no panel.

    title             What the panel is titled. Defaults to the policy's name.
    height            Number, required when ListsPage is given, checked at registration. The panel's usable height.

    Construct(client)
              Called once, from BuildListsPolicyUI, once everything else on the page exists.

    ListSelection(client, cfgid, listid)
              The selected list changed, or the lists were refreshed. LISTID is nil with no list selected. CFGID is
              pol.currentid and is nil for a policy with no config.

    MemberSelection(client, cfgid, listid, uid, pos)
              The selected member changed. UID is nil with no member selected, and UID and POS are both nil while any tab
              but Members is showing. POS is the member's place in the policy's Order. On a tethered list UID and POS are
              the main's even when an alt row was clicked.

              Order is ListSelection, then ConfigListSelection, then MemberSelection.

              The panel's title dims when nothing inside it is enabled, so the policy's own SetEnabled calls in these hooks
              are what light it.

Lists page, Config tab
    ConfigConstruct(client) -> height
              The policy's panel titled with its name under List Configuration, for the options it declares in its
              descriptor's listoptions. Called once, straight after Construct, and answers how tall what it built is. An
              option calls konfer:ListsPending():Set("p." .. option, value) when the user changes it, which puts it among
              the tab's unsaved changes and brings up the save bar, and Watch(widget, "p." .. option) once so the widget
              shows the changed colour.

    ConfigListSelection(client, cfgid, listid)
              Called after ListSelection on every list selection, and again after every refresh of the lists. LISTID is nil
              with no list selected. Set the widgets from konfer:ListsPending():Value("p." .. option), which is what is on
              screen, unsaved changes included, without throwing when there is no list. Refill any choice of lists here,
              because lists come and go.

              Saving the tab writes the list's own fields, sortorder, def_rank, strictcfilter, strictrfilter, extralist,
              tethered and altdisp, and the policy's whole block of options on the list, and sends them all in one CHLST.
              The policy reads an option with pol:ListOption(cfgid, listid, name).

Lists page, the policy's own tabs
  ListsTabs   An array of tabs after Members and Config. Each is { name, text, adminonly, owneronly, wholepage, Construct,
              ListSelection }, as documented in Policy.lua. A tab has no save bar, nothing on it being held for saving. A
              wholepage tab covers the whole page, the column of lists hidden while it is up, and chooses its list for
              itself.

    Construct(client)
              Called once, when the policy's panels are built. CLIENT is the tab's content frame.

    ListSelection(client, cfgid, listid)
              Called beside ListsPage's ListSelection, after it, with the same arguments, whether or not the policy has a
              ListsPage.

              EP/GP's Journal is one, a wholepage tab: a list's awards of EP and decays, newest first, the list chosen at
              its foot, each naming on hover everybody it reached.

  InsertUI    { Construct(parent, owner, where) }, the row the insert popup reserves for the policy. Built once per policy
              and swapped as the active one changes. InsertInfo(cfgid, listid, where) -> pdata is read from it when somebody
              is picked. Suicide Kings puts Insert randomly there.

  ImportList(cfgid, listid)
              Put up the policy's own dialog for importing members into LISTID of CFGID. Called by the tab's Import button,
              which is disabled for a policy without it. What an import takes in and where the people land are the
              policy's; the platform supplies the parts that are the same everywhere:
                konfer:ParseNames(text) -> names | nil, why    names separated by commas, or a JSON array, in order
                cfg:ImportNames(listid, names) -> uids, missing   the config's uids for them, and those it has nobody for
                cfg:ImportGuildRank(listid, rank) -> uids      a guild rank's members, made users where they are not yet
                konfer:CurrentListBox(dlg, ypos), konfer:ShowCurrentList(box, cfgid, listid)
              The two cfg calls take each person once and, on a tethered list, an alt for their main.

  AddMissing(cfgid, listid)
              The same for the tab's Add missing button, which is also disabled out of a group.
              cfg:MissingFromList(listid, raidonly) -> uids is who could be on LISTID and is not, the raid's only with RAIDONLY.

Raid page
  RaidPage    A table, what the policy shows and does on the Raid page, everybody in the group (Raid.lua). Each row is the
              platform's up to the end of the name, the markers and the roles packed at the far end of the Name column, and
              the policy's columns follow it. Its hooks are only called with a config in hand. The fields, as documented in
              Policy.lua, are solo, title(cfgid), columns, Columns(cfgid), Row(cfgid, raider), buttons and
              Removed(cfgid, uid).

              The list is built once with every policy's columns, so a policy declares all it may ever show in columns,
              and Columns answers which show for a config and which the list starts sorted on. Row answers a number for
              each. The policy's buttons stand on the second row under the list, the platform's Remove among them. Broadcast's
              tooltip and Remove's question are words of the policy's own (Strings: RAID_BROADCAST, RAID_REMOVE, and
              RAID_REMOVE_MANY for the Selected dropdown's Remove taking more than one).

              EP/GP shows EP, GP and PR and DKP the balance, on the list being worked on (CurrentList), and PUG what
              tonight has cost each raider, with Penalty, Reward and End raid, keeping the page up out of a group.

  RaidTabs    An array of tabs after Raiders. Each is { name, text, hsplit, adminonly, owneronly, Construct, Refresh }. A
              tab has no save bar. Refresh is called whenever the page is drawn. PUG's Reserves is one.

Loot page, Item editor tab
  AssignPage  A table, of what the policy changes about the Loot page's Assign loot tab.

    hide              What on the tab the policy has no use for, as a set. rank is Constraints' guild rank filter and its -
                      button, the strict filters under it moving up into its room. lists is the Lists panel and forcebid
                      the panel of Force bid and Force retract under Candidates, which takes whatever room they leave. PUG
                      hides all three.

    firstroll         { text, tooltip }, for a policy that hides bids: the bids button reads TEXT and opens the item's
                      first roll while one is owed (FirstRoll), dark otherwise. PUG's reads Reserves. Label (cfgid, ilink)
                      -> text, tooltip names it for the item selected instead, and Open (cfgid, ilink) -> opened, asked
                      first when it is pressed, opens something of the policy's own in the roll's place: Loot Council's
                      reads Council vote and opens the vote, or Roll-off.

  ItemsPage   A table. With a Construct, the policy gets a panel titled with its name in the left column under the Delete
              button. Without one there is no panel there at all. Who an item goes to, its next drop and award list
              included, is Item instructions', the platform's, and what a policy keeps on an item is its one opaque pdata
              field.

    hide              The rows of Item instructions the policy has no use for, as a set of the item fields they set: rank,
                      list, nextuser, autodel, awardlist. Those under a hidden row move up into its room. PUG hides rank,
                      list and awardlist.

    Construct(client, changed) -> height
              Called once, from BuildLootPolicyUI. Answers how tall what it built is, which the panel is made to hold
              exactly, the Items list above it taking the rest of the column. CHANGED puts what the user has written into
              IINFO among the editor's unsaved changes. Watch each widget with konfer:ItemsPending():Watch(widget, field, ...)
              so it shows the changed colour.

    ItemSelection(client, cfgid, itemid, iinfo)
              Called when an item is selected in the item list, when Ignore item or either auto assign option is toggled
              (both reset what the policy shows), and when the item list is refreshed. IINFO is the item's edit buffer, or
              nil with no item selected. Set and enable the widgets from IINFO alone and write edits into it.

              Saving the editor writes the item's fixed set of fields and CHITM carries them. IINFO.pdata is the policy's
              own, any value it likes, stored on the item, sent with it and never read by the platform. EP/GP keeps an
              item's custom GP there.

Users page
  UsersPage   A table.

    title             What the policy's section is titled. Defaults to the policy's name.
    compact           True to give the section the room the page can spare: the page's buttons go under the user list, two
                      rows of two without Guild import, and User info has no separator between the config's view of a user
                      and the character's. PUG's, for its notes.

    Construct(client, changed, width) -> height
              With this, the policy gets a section under the page's buttons, or under User info and the lists on a compact
              page, the column wide, WIDTH inside the panel's rings, and answers how tall what it built is, which the panel
              is made to hold exactly (PUG's
              notes). Answering nothing, the section runs down to the foot of the page, for one holding something that takes
              whatever room there is. Called once,
              from BuildUsersPolicyUI. Without it there is no panel there at all. The panel's title dims while no user is
              selected, as the page's own do. What the policy keeps about a user is their entry's pdata (section 6), and the
              section edits it among the page's unsaved changes: CHANGED(pdata) says what it now is, the save bar writes it
              with the rest and sends it in the one MDUSR, and konfer:UsersPending():Watch(widget, "pdata") gives a widget
              the changed colour.

    UserSelection(client, cfgid, uid, pdata)
              The selected user changed, or the section has just come up. UID is nil when nobody is selected. PDATA is what
              is kept about them as it stands on screen, unsaved changes included, a copy the policy may change as it likes.

    CreateConstruct(dlg, ypos) -> ypos
              Adds the policy's fields to the create user dialog, under the standard ones and the platform's Raid enchanter
              box, at DLG.x from YPOS down, and returns where the next field would go. Called once, when the dialog is built,
              which is the first time Create is clicked while this policy is active. dlg.entry is a fresh table every time
              the dialog opens, and each field resets itself on the dialog's OnShow and writes into dlg.entry.pdata, which
              the new user's entry keeps as their pdata. dlg.shared holds the standard fact widgets (pprof, sprof and the
              rest).

Config page, the policy's own tabs
  ConfigTabs  An array of tabs after Loot and Admin. Each is { name, text, adminonly, owneronly, Construct, Refresh }, as
              documented in Policy.lua. A policy with loot options of its own puts them here, the Loot tab being the
              platform's and having no room for a policy's section.

    Construct(client, pending)
              Called once, when the policy's panels are built. PENDING is the tab's unsaved changes, the same object
              konfer:PolicyTabPending(pol, name) answers, holding the policy's own settings as "p." and the name. Saving
              sends them as one PSSET.

    Refresh(client, cfgid)
              Put CFGID's values back, from PENDING so an unsaved change still shows. CFGID is the config picked in the
              Admin tab's list, or nil with none picked.

              EP/GP's Advanced tab, the owner's only, holds the price of gear.

Config page, Admin tab
  CreateConfigUI(parent) -> height
              Builds the policy's own config options into PARENT, the panel titled with the policy's name on the Admin tab.
              Called once, from BuildConfigPolicyUI. Return the height used, from the top of PARENT to the bottom of the
              lowest widget, and the panel is made exactly that tall. Return nil and the panel ends level with the
              Co-admins panel. The Admin tab is every admin's; within it, the owner and co-admin controls enable only for
              the owner. Nothing on the tab is written when it is changed: an option calls konfer:AdminPending():Set with
              "p." and the setting's name, and saving sends the policy's as one PSSET.

  RefreshConfigUI(cfgid)
              Put CFGID's values back into those widgets. CFGID is the config the Admin tab has selected, which need not be
              the one the window has in hand. Called when the selection changes and when the roll lists do.

  EnableConfigUI(onoff, cfgid)
              The Admin tab gained or lost a selected config, CFGID or nil. The panel dims only its title, so enable or
              disable the widgets here.

  ConfigLootPage  { hide }, what on the Config page's Loot tab the policy has no use for, as a set. bids is everything
              about bidding: Bid options, after which Roll options says what becomes of an item nobody rolls for, the
              heading and the enchanters to assign it to, and Enable silent bidding in General options. PUG hides it.
              Any announcement on the Announcements menu may be named by its setting as well, ann_winners_guild for
              Winners in guild chat, and it is then off the menu and never made, whatever the config says, every one of
              them being read through cfg:Announces. PUG hides that one and ann_missing, Raiders not on current list.
              announce_guild is In guild chat under Announce loot, loot the config says to announce there then being
              announced nowhere (konfer:LootAnnouncedTo). PUG hides it.

  AdminPage  { hide }, the platform's panels on the Admin tab the policy has no use for, as a set. options is Options, the
              default list, guild rank filtering and rank order, and without it the policy's own panel starts at the top of
              the right hand column. PUG hides it.

  AdminPanel  { title, Construct(client) }, a second panel of the policy's own under the first, running down to the foot
              of Co-admins, there only when CreateConfigUI answered a height. Built once, straight after it. Its edits may
              be held in konfer:AdminPending() as the first panel's are or saved as they are made, which is the policy's
              choice, and RefreshConfigUI and EnableConfigUI cover it too. EP/GP's and DKP's awards are one, saved as made.

The policy's command
  Commands    An array of { word, args, help, Run } (Policy.lua), words after the policy's slash command beside the
              platform's. Run(input, pol) is only ever called with a config in hand, the policy being the active one, and
              /<command> help lists them after the platform's. A word the platform answers stays the platform's.

Every page
  GuildRanksChanged(ranks)
              The guild's ranks changed, or this character's guild did. RANKS is { { text = K.NONE_STR, value = 0 },
              { text = rankname, value = index }, ... }, the list the platform's own rank dropdowns use. Refill any rank
              choice of the policy's own, keeping the selection with SetValue(old, true) so it does not read as an edit.
              Called for every registered policy, including ones switched off and never built, so check the widgets exist.

  PositionText(cfgid, listid, uid, pos) -> string
              How the policy words a member's standing, POS being their place in its Order. Called through PolicyCall on
              the Lists page's members, the Loot page's candidates, the Users page's lists, the raid announcement of a bid
              and the Lists page's Announce. The default is tostring(pos). A tethered alt's row on the Lists page shows
              nothing whatever this returns.

  ValueText(cfgid, listid, uid) -> string
              What a member is worth on a list, in brackets after their name in the foyer's Overview, their place in the
              policy's Order already standing before it. Nothing where it answers nil, and a policy whose standing is its
              place gives none. EP/GP says "PR 1.71" and DKP "3121 DKP".

  UserTooltip(cfgid, listid, uid) -> array of { left, right }
              What the policy says about a member on a list, under a blank line on their tooltip on the Lists page and
              among the Loot page's candidates. Nothing where the tooltip is about no list. EP/GP and DKP give the
              member's numbers and their lifetime totals.

  ItemTooltip(cfgid, ilink) -> { title, lines }
              What the active policy says about an item, under a blank line on every item tooltip the game draws, with
              the config it has in hand, a raider's copy included, the title optional. EP/GP and DKP give its price as
              each kind of bid the config takes, or at auction the least each may bid. PUG names who reserved it.

  BroadcastArriving(cfgid) -> before, BroadcastArrived(cfgid, before)
              Either side of a broadcast replacing a copy this client already holds, for telling the player what it
              changed for them. EP/GP and DKP keep the player's numbers on each list and say which came out different.

  standingfont, standingwidth
              The font PositionText is drawn in and how many characters the widest standing has, for the standing column
              on the Lists page's members and the Loot page's candidates (konfer:StandingColumn). A width of 0 is a
              policy with no standing to show, whose PositionText answers "", and the column takes no room. PUG says 0.

  Strings     Table of words the platform draws, keyed by name, values already localised. pol:Str(word) returns the
              policy's word, else the platform's neutral one, else the key itself so a typo shows on screen. The words are

                AWARD_BUTTON     the button that applies an award to a list member by hand. The platform draws none
                                 itself; SK's own Lists panel uses it.
                AWARD_EXTRALIST  the label on the list option naming the extra list an award also lands on.
                AWARD_ITEMLIST   the item editor option naming the list an auto assigned item's award is applied on.
                AWARD_ROLL       the raid warning opening a roll that carries an award, taking list name, item and seconds.
                AWARD_UNDO       the undo dialog's question.
                AWARD_AUTO       what the master looter is told the award will do, when asked whether to go through
                                 with an item's instructions, taking the list. The winner is already named.
                COST             the caption of the cost in the costing box, saying what it is counted in:
                                 "Cost (GP)" for EP/GP.
                BID_STANDING     where a bidder stands, in the raid announcement of their bid, taking PositionText's.
                ANNOUNCE_RAIDERS the heading of what the Lists page's Announce says to the raid, taking the list name.
                                 The mod's name goes in front and the raiders after, each with PositionText's.
                ANNOUNCE_ALL     the same for Announce all, whose members carry no standing.
                NOT_ON_LIST      what the master looter is told before the raiders not on the list loot is being given
                                 out on, taking the list name.
                HELP_STANDING    the help whisper's line for the standing whisper, taking the asker's name and the word.
                                 PUG's says it shows what tonight's wins have cost, PUG answering that whisper itself.

  Whispers    What the policy calls each whisper command a raider can send, keyed by the platform's name for it. What each
              does is the platform's (konfer.std_whispers, copied onto every policy); Suicide Kings answers "suicide" and
              "position" where the platform would say "standing". Each kind of bid is a whisper of its own, "bidM" to "bidV",
              "!ms" or "!os" and their aliases, an amount following where bids take one; a kind is the platform's, and so
              are its words, which each locale lists and which answer the English words besides. A key that is none of the
              platform's is a whisper of the policy's own, its handler put in pol.whispers after registration: PUG's
              "reserves", "!sr".


5.  Loot
--------

A list is always required to bid, whatever the policy: the item's own list, else the config's def_list, else the list
selected. Who wins is the policy's. The loot engine asks it through cfg:PolicyCall, which pcalls the hook and falls back to
the default when it is missing or raises, telling the player once a session through pol:err. Only the master looter ranks;
everybody else shows what it sends.

  Order(cfgid, listid, uids, loot) -> uids
              The list's members in display and priority order. Defaults to the stored array. cfg:ListPosition walks it, so
              it is what every position the platform shows or announces is counted in. LOOT is true for the Loot page's
              candidates, who may be ordered differently from the list: EP/GP breaks a tie on PR by EP there alone, and
              everywhere else by name, as DKP does a tie on balance. PUG orders by name alone.

  BidAmounts(cfgid) -> boolean
              Whether a bid names an amount as well as a kind, "!ms 150". The raid is told as bids open, which puts a box to
              type it in beside Bid on item, and a bid without one is refused; where it is false an amount whispered after
              the word is ignored. With silent bidding off the amounts go to the raid with the bidders and into the
              announcements, and with it on nobody but the master looter sees them. DKP says yes when it runs auctions.

  RankBidders(cfgid, listid, bidders, ilink) -> bidders
              BIDDERS arrive by .tier, the place the config's Bid options give their kind (lower is better), within a tier
              highest .amount first where bids take one, then in Order's order, each with .uid, .kind, .tier, .amount, .idx
              and .standing. Return the same tables, reordered or thinned, winner first. The master looter calls it afresh
              on every bid, change of bid and retraction and whenever the list moves, and BIDER carries the result whole to
              the raid. Bidders the policy cannot tell apart share a .tie; when bids close with the first two sharing one,
              those tied roll off, main spec, and the winner has the bid they made (VIA "bid"), for what they bid.

  MayBid(cfgid, listid, uid, kind, amount, ilink) -> allowed, why
              Whether UID may bid KIND on ILINK at all, for AMOUNT where bids name one, asked after the platform's own checks
              and again when a bid changes, WHY being whispered back when not. EP/GP refuses a member below its minimum EP
              when the config says so, and DKP one who cannot afford the bid or the item.

  RollAwards(cfgid, listid, ilink) -> boolean
              Whether an open roll on ILINK carries an award for its winner as a bid would. Shift when opening the roll asks
              for the other. Without it a roll awards nothing and the winner simply gets the item.

  RollCeiling(cfgid, uid, kind) -> top
              In a roll that carries an award, the most UID may roll of KIND out of its hundred. PUG's decay answers it.

  RollPriority(cfgid, uid, kind, ilink, awards) -> number
              Where UID stands among the rollers of their kind's tier, ahead of what they rolled, lower first, in any roll,
              AWARDS saying whether it carries an award. PUG puts an item's soft reservers first, and in a roll that carries
              an award the fewest +1s.

  MayRoll(cfgid, uid, kind, ilink) -> allowed, why
              Whether UID may roll on ILINK at all, asked as their first roll in it arrives, WHY whispered back when not,
              and of every candidate with KIND nil: the Candidates are those it allows who meet the item's Constraints,
              whatever the policy. PUG keeps everybody but the reservers out of an item's first roll.

  FirstRoll(cfgid, ilink) -> boolean
              A roll owed to a few, those MayRoll lets in, before anybody else. While it is owed the bids button opens it
              under the name AssignPage.firstroll gives it, and Open roll is dark. Only a policy that takes no bids has one.
              PUG's are its soft reserves, where its config says they keep others out, the button reading Reserves. Loot
              Council's are an item the council has yet to vote on, which the button opens the vote on instead
              (AssignPage.firstroll.Open), and claimants the council left level.

  FirstRollUnanswered(cfgid, ilink)
              Nobody rolled in the first roll. The item is back on offer rather than going where an item nobody rolls for
              goes, and every client is told, EROLL carrying it, so its Candidates follow. PUG lets everybody in.

  RollNotice(cfgid, ilink) -> text
              Said to the raid as a roll opens, after the line opening it. PUG names the item's reservers.

  ItemClaim(cfgid, ilink) -> claimed, uid, award
              An item spoken for, never rolled or bid on. Claimed for UID, somebody who can have it, it is put to the master
              looter as an item's own instructions are, before them, and otherwise it is theirs to hand out, Open roll and
              Open bids staying dark on it. AWARD makes the win an award on the list the item is given out on, the policy's
              Award and HistoryEntry with VIA "assign"; without it the win is the platform's A row. PUG's hard reserves
              answer it, each claiming its item while whoever it is for is in the group, and leaving it to the master
              looter, a roll included, while they are not. Loot Council's decisions answer it with an award. A policy whose
              claim has just been decided puts the item on offer again with konfer:OfferLoot (section 10).

  Kinds of bid and roll are one table, ROLL_KINDS in Loot.lua, and are not a policy's to declare. Every policy has all four,
  "M" main spec, "O" off spec, "T" transmog and "V" vanity, and a config takes main spec and whichever of the others its Bid
  options switch on. Which kind outranks which is the config's too. Rolls resolve by kind, main spec's range first, highest
  in a range winning. The winner's kind reaches Award and HistoryEntry.

  The win (Loot.lua, THE WIN)
              A win is somebody winning a bid or a roll, or an item's own instructions naming somebody. The master looter is
              always asked whether to go through with instructions, and yes is that person winning the item exactly as a
              bid is won; no leaves the item on offer as any item is.

              A policy with costing puts every win to the master looter in the costing box, with the policy window standing
              aside: the item, the winner, the list the award is charged on as a dropdown they may change, and what it costs
              as up to four digits, filled in with the policy's price for them to type over, with Full price, 25%, 50% and
              75% off and Free. Each charges its share of what the box says, the policy's price when it is emptied. There is
              no Cancel and no closing it. The box is shown even where the price is 0, a plain roll's win included.

              A roll's win or an item's instructions may be put on -None-, no list at all, which is where one carrying no
              award starts: nothing is charged, the cost reads 0 and can be neither typed in nor discounted, Full price reads
              Award item, and the history row is the platform's, the roll's kind or A. A won bid, a roll-off included, is never offered -None-, Free
              being how it is given for nothing.

              Pressing a button, or for a policy without costing the win itself, decides it: the award at that cost, its
              history row, and the announcement to the raid and the guild. Then the item is handed over, where the config
              auto-assigns loot when bids close and the winner is in the group. A policy without costing asks the master
              looter first, "shall I auto-loot this to X?", and Cancel leaves the handing over to them. A hand-out the game
              refuses is delivery failing, not the win, which stands. Either way the item leaves the loot list.

              Nobody winning, nobody having bid or rolled, is no award and nobody is asked: a roll where the config says to
              try one, and otherwise the item goes, where loot is auto-assigned, to the master looter if it is a BoE and
              the config says so, else to the first of tonight's enchanters who can have it, else to the master looter.

              An item the item editor sets to go to the enchanter or the master looter is not won. It is offered like any
              item whatever its quality, and picking it asks the master looter whether to hand it over. Yes does only that,
              with no award, no costing box and nothing announced, and writes the platform's D or A row. No leaves it on
              offer. One for the enchanter that no enchanter can have goes to the master looter.

  Cost(cfgid, listid, uid, ilink, kind, bids) -> number
              What the win costs, the number the costing box starts at, captioned with the policy's COST word. BIDS, for an
              item won by bidding, is every bid as it stood when bids closed, winner first, each { uid, kind, tier, amount },
              which is what a price that comes from the bidding is worked out from; nil for a roll or instructions. Only a
              policy with costing is asked, and nil is 0. EP/GP answers ItemPrice, which is the item's own price where the
              item editor gives it one. DKP answers the same at a fixed price, and at auction what the winner bid, or the
              next highest bid of the same tier where the config says so.

  Award(cfgid, listid, uid, ilink, rlist, chain, nocmd, norefresh, kind, cost) -> { { listid = id, token = anything }, ... }
              UID has won ILINK on list LISTID. Apply that to the standings and return one entry per list actually changed,
              each with an opaque TOKEN that will come back to UndoAward. Return an empty table, or nil, when nothing
              changed.

              RLIST is cfg:CreateRaidList(listid), the members of the list who are in the raid, reserved, or have a
              tethered alt in the raid, in list order. CHAIN says the award also lands on the list's extra list, a
              platform option of every list, which a loot award does and a correction by hand does not. Suicide Kings
              suicides the winner on both lists and EP/GP charges them on both, each list a move of its own. NOCMD means the
              award arrived from another admin, so record no event. NOREFRESH means the caller is replaying a batch and will
              refresh once at the end. ILINK is nil for an award made by hand. KIND is the kind of bid or roll that won, and
              nil for none. COST is what the master looter charged in the costing box, nil where the policy's own price
              stands.

              cfg:AwardItem is the platform's side and is what the loot engine calls when a win is decided, always with the
              active policy's current config and CHAIN true. It answers (moves, ok), OK false when the hook failed, and then
              nothing is done: the costing box stays up, or the item is back on offer. Unless NOCMD, it pushes one undo
              record for the whole award. SK also calls it from its Lists panel and from its SULST handler.

              A policy that sends an event here sends it from Award, through its own AddEvent, and skips it when NOCMD is
              set, and its handler for that event calls cfg:AwardItem with nocmd = true (Policies/SK/Comms.lua).

  UndoAward(cfgid, listid, uid, ilink, token, nocmd)
              Reverse an award on one list, TOKEN being what Award returned for LISTID. Send the policy's undo event unless
              NOCMD. cfg:UndoAward calls it for each move, newest first, and only then, when not NOCMD and ILINK is set, puts
              ILINK back on the loot list and writes a "U" history row. A policy whose token cannot be sent as it is must
              turn it into something its handler can rebuild.

  HistoryEntry(cfgid, via, listid, uid, ilink, kind, cost) -> how, pdata
              The loot history row for an award, COST as Award has it. A row's HOW is upper case for the platform's own ways
              an item leaves (a plain roll, disenchanting, a BoE to the master looter, one the master looter kept, an undo)
              and lower case for the policy's. Every row names
              the list it was given out on, the platform's field, false for none. PDATA beside it is kept by whoever owns HOW
              and never read by the other. VIA is "bid", "roll" or "assign", and this is called before the award, so it
              records how things stood when the item was won. A HOW that does not start with a lower case letter is refused
              and the award goes unrecorded. PDATA travels in LHADD, a broadcast and a sync as it was returned. SK answers
              "s", "r" and "a" with the position; EP/GP answers "g", "r" and "a" with { gp, kind }; DKP "b", "r" and "a"
              with { cost, kind }.

  HistoryText(cfgid, how, listid, pdata, colour) -> howtext, whattext
              What the History tab draws in the How and What columns for one of this policy's rows. An empty string leaves the
              column to the platform. With COLOUR it is what the list draws, without it plain words, which the list sorts by
              and an export carries.

  WinText(cfgid, how, listid, pdata, who, ilink) -> text
              How the raid and the guild are told WHO won ILINK with an award on LISTID, without the mod's name, which the
              platform puts in front. Nil or an empty string gives the platform's plain line. SK says
              "Oswald[3] won ... on the "Main Raid" list. Grats!".

  EncounterEnded(enc)
              A boss fight ended, won or lost, as KoreLoot's encounter_ended hands it on. ENC is { id, name, difficulty,
              size, killed, when }, the game's encounter id, the fight's name, its difficulty id, the group's size, whether
              the boss died, and when, in UTC seconds. Every client in the fight hears it, so a policy that acts on one checks
              who it is for itself. Called for the active policy only. EP/GP and DKP offer the master looter the boss's award
              on a kill (Points.lua).

  StatusChanged(name, status, onoff)
              NAME, somebody in the group, gained one of the marks the raid frames show, ONOFF true, or lost it, false, as
              KoreParty's status_changed hands it on. STATUS is one of KRP's STATUS_ constants. Joining a group gains
              everybody's. Called for the active policy only. PUG warns its player of a main tank they rated a terrible tank.

  rl.journal  (a list field the policy fills)
              The policy's record of what it did to a list that is not in the loot history, EP/GP's and DKP's awards and
              decays and DKP's zero-sum shares. The platform copies it with the list and never reads it; the policy fills it
              from its own events, so every admin's copy is filled alike, and sends it in its ListInfo, as EP/GP does
              rl.lastdecay.

  A policy with no Award does nothing to standings when someone wins, and one with no HistoryEntry writes no history for it.
  The item is still given.

  EP/GP and DKP share earning points, decaying them, the journal, the price of an item, the award, adjust and decay
  dialogs, the Lists page's panel, the Journal and Advanced tabs, the item editor's price and the import dialogs, all of
  it in Policies/Points.lua (konfer.points). Each hands points:Setup its own numbers, words and event names before it
  registers, and points:Events its registered self, and keeps what is its own: how it ranks, and what winning does.

  PUG and Loot Council share the tally, what tonight's wins have cost each raider: the kind of raid, simple, +1 or decay,
  and what it counts by, the raid that keeps the counts on the config, the hooks that make a roll cost (RollAwards,
  RollCeiling, RollPriority, Award, UndoAward, HistoryEntry, HistoryText, WinText), the Raid page's columns and buttons,
  the question at login about a raid never ended, and the standing whisper, all of it in Policies/Tally.lua
  (konfer.tally). Each hands tally:Setup its own event names before it registers, PUG its reserves as a priority ahead
  of the +1s, and tally:Events its registered self; any hook a policy defines itself is its own, Loot Council wrapping
  the tally's for the wins its council decides.


6.  Data a policy works with
----------------------------

A policy has no saved variables of its own. Its configs are konfer:PolicyConfigs(pol), frdb[handle].configs keyed by config
id, and each is an object: its fields are the data the database saves, and everything that acts on one is a method from
konfer.configmethods (section 10). Data.txt says where every piece of state lives and why; in short:

  cfg.settings   The platform's settings, a proxy over the two strings cfg.opts and cfg.vals (konfer.copts, konfer.cvals).
  cfg.psettings  [handle] = the policy's own settings, declared in its defaults, read and written with pol:Setting and
                 pol:ChangeSetting, and sent whole as PSSET.
  cfg.pdata      [handle] = the policy's own data on the config, declared in its configdata and read with
                 cfg:PolicyData(handle). Whole only when the config travels whole; between admins, changed an entry at a
                 time by the policy's own events. EP/GP's and DKP's awards.
  cfg.uids       [uid] = { name, enchanter, frozen, pdata }, positional through konfer.CFGUSER_*. Enchanter and frozen are
                 the platform's, in every policy. pdata is what the policy keeps about the user, any value it likes,
                 read with cfg:UserPData and written with cfg:SetUserPData, and sent whole in MDUSR, a broadcast and a sync.
                 The facts about the character are Kore's, in KU.users.
  cfg.lists      [listid] = { name, sortorder, def_rank, strictcfilter, strictrfilter, extralist, tethered, altdisp, users,
                 standings, journal, poptions, ... }. users is the ordered array of uids, whose order is the policy's to
                 decide (SetMemberInfo). standings is rl.standings[uid], made by pol:Standings. What the policy says about a
                 member or a list travels as pdata through MemberInfo / SetMemberInfo and ListInfo / SetListInfo.
                 poptions[handle] = the policy's own options on the list, declared in its listoptions, read with
                 pol:ListOption and edited on the Lists page's Config tab, sent whole in that tab's CHLST.
  cfg.items      [itemid] = the platform's item instructions and pdata, the policy's own.

Every change to a config that other admins must see goes through an event, because the checksum is a running XOR over the
event stream (section 7). pol:AddEvent(cfgid, cmd, ...) sends one of the policy's own.


7.  The wire
------------

Every policy is its own endpoint, and they all talk under the mod's one prefix, KONFER. RegisterPolicy registers the policy
with KoreComms and gives it an endpoint name, its handle, which every message it sends carries.

A Kore message is a version letter, a checksum and a payload Kore never looks into, and nothing in it is this mod's. Kore is
generic and Konfer sits on top of it, so everything about a message that is Konfer's is said inside that payload, in the
four values every one of them begins with:

  konfer.protocol   this mod's protocol version, compared by the receiver before it reads anything else. Higher than ours
                    means arguments we cannot read, so KC.OldProtoDialog and nothing further.
  endpoint          a policy's handle, or konfer.CMD_MOD ("*") for the mod itself.
  cmd               the command.
  cfgid             the config it is about, or false, so the slot is there whether or not it is filled.

konfer:MakeSends (Policy.lua) puts all four there. Kore gives every endpoint SendAM, SendGuildAM and SendWhisperAM and
nothing else, and MakeSends stands this mod's own in front of those three and adds the three C variants, which are a
policy's alone: a config belongs to one policy and the mod has no current config of its own. Each also tells Kore whether
the message is worth holding while the game refuses addon messages, which Kore cannot work out for itself.

konfer:AcceptMessage is where a policy this client does not run, or one the user has switched off, stops. It runs on the
endpoint value once Kore has deserialised, because nothing about a Konfer message is legible before then. A message about a
config this policy does not hold is dropped just after it.

One prefix rather than one each because the game's cap on them is the client's and is shared by every addon the player runs,
and because every policy ships inside Konfer and registers at load, so a client that hears one hears them all whatever they
are addressed to.

Handler tables
  pol.ihandlers and pol.ehandlers are the policy's own copies of konfer.std_ihandlers and konfer.std_ehandlers, made at
  registration. A policy's Comms.lua adds commands only it knows, replaces a standard one it wants handled differently, or
  sets one to nil to stop answering it. None of this touches any other policy. Event names are five characters by
  convention and stay the policy's own (SULST, SUNDO, SUMOV, KEGEP, KEGGP), never a shared AWARD.

  ihandler(konfer, sender, proto, pol, cmd, cfg, ...)
              Immediate raid traffic, recorded nowhere. No admin check, no event id, no checksum.

  ehandler(konfer, live, sender, proto, pol, cmd, cfg, ...)
              Part of the config's event stream. LIVE is true for an event that arrived on its own and false for one
              replayed in a sync batch. A handler redraws only when LIVE, a batch redrawing once when it ends.

  The first argument is the konfer addon rather than the policy, because the handlers are the mod's code whichever policy's
  copy of the table holds them, and POL is the policy the command named. A handler wanting the policy of some other config
  still asks konfer:GetPolicy(cfgid).

Dispatch  (Comms.lua AcceptMessage, then commdispatch, then policy_commdispatch)
  In this order. A payload that did not deserialise is dropped. One from a protocol above ours raises KC.OldProtoDialog and
  is read no further. One for a policy this client does not run, or one the user has switched off, is thrown away. One named
  CMD_MOD goes to the mod's own handlers and a command that is not one of them raises KC.OldProtoDialog. Then, for the
  policy the message named: one naming a config this policy does not hold is dropped, ihandlers included. An ihandler
  runs and that is all. A command that is not an ehandler either raises KC.OldProtoDialog. An event with no config is
  dropped. A non-admin drops anything not sent as a user event. Everybody drops an event from an unknown sender or from
  someone who is not an admin of the config. While the config is syncing an admin also drops an event whose previous event
  id does not match what it last had from that sender, or whose checksum before does not match ours, and otherwise folds it
  into the checksum and records its id. An event whose arguments do not decode is dropped. Then the ehandler runs with LIVE
  true.

Sending
  pol:AddEvent(cfgid, cmd, ...)
              Records and sends an event of the policy's. Only an admin's changes are events: for anybody else it records
              and sends nothing. Otherwise it XORs the event into the checksum, queues it for every other admin's sync while
              the config is syncing, and sends it through the config's policy's CSendAM. cfg:AddEvent and cfg:AdminEvent are
              the platform's own.

  While retail refuses addon messages (an encounter, a challenge mode run, a PvP match or restricted addon chat) Kore holds
  every event, and sync, recovery and broadcasts, sending them in order once the restriction lifts, and drops everything
  else, chat included, telling the player once.

  pol:CSendAM(cfgid, cmd, prio, ...)
              Sends about CFGID to the group this character is in. Out of a group, it goes as CSendChannelAM sends.

  pol:CSendChannelAM(cfgid, cmd, prio, ...)
              Sends about CFGID to the guild when cfg.channel is guild and this character is guilded, else to the group,
              in a group or not. The sync request, the users refresh and the loot history broadcast. With CSendAM, the only
              reader of cfg.channel.

  SendAM, CSendGuildAM, SendGuildAM, CSendWhisperAM, SendWhisperAM, SendText, SendGuildText, SendWhisper, SendWarning
              This mod's own, put there by konfer:MakeSends over the three Kore gives every endpoint. Each writes the four
              value preamble and tells Kore whether to hold the message. The non-C ones send false for the config.

The standard set every policy gets

  ihandlers   ACKRS ALOOT BICAN BIDCL BIDER BIDME BIDOP BIDRT BIREM CLOOT EROLL FLTCH FSYNC GSYNC LISEL LLSEL MSYNC OLOOT
              OROLL PROLL RCACK RCOVR REQRS RFUSR RKIND RROLL RSUSR RSYNC SYNAK TROLL XROLL.
              RCOVR and RCACK are recovery, and are the only ones here naming no config at all: the owner asking for one back
              may hold it under an id the admin answering does not, or under none, so RCOVR names it by name and finds it in
              POL. BIDER carries the bidders as the policy ranked them. REQRS and ACKRS are the raid's settings, who is
              reserved and tonight's disenchanters. FSYNC and MSYNC are how a full or partial sync arrives, and MSYNC replays
              each event through the config's policy's ehandlers with LIVE false.

  ehandlers   CHITM CHLST CHSET CHUSR CPLST DMLST IMLST LHADD MDUSR MKADM MKITM MKLST MKUSR MVLST MVUSR PSSET RMADM RMITM
              RMLST RMUSR SMLST.
              IMLST and SMLST carry the policy's pdata for a member and a whole list, MDUSR a user's whole entry, the
              policy's pdata for them included. CHITM carries the item's instructions and its pdata. CHSET carries the
              platform's settings whole, PSSET a policy's. LHADD carries the row's HOW, its list and the pdata of whoever
              owns HOW. MKADM and RMADM are user events, sent only by the owner, so raiders know who the admins are.

  The mod's own, not copied onto policies, are DEBUG CINFO CIACK BCAST CSYNC. CINFO and CIACK are the character info
  check, which is this mod's whole and lives in CharInfo.lua. BCAST and CSYNC are the mod's because the receiver may hold
  neither the config nor the policy: BCAST carries the config's policy and channel, and CSYNC is every config this client
  syncs, across every policy, in one message.


8.  What RegisterPolicy puts on the policy
------------------------------------------

All of these are assigned unconditionally, so a descriptor field or method of the same name is silently replaced.

  prefix            handle .. "_".
  external          True when registered after Init.lua loaded.
  order             The descriptor's order, plus POLICY_EXTERNAL_BASE when external.
  raid, party, bg   Defaulted where nil.
  StandingFields    {} where nil.
  ihandlers         Copy of konfer.std_ihandlers.
  ehandlers         Copy of konfer.std_ehandlers.
  whispers          Copy of konfer.std_whispers, with whisperwords and whisperword, the words the policy answers to.
  endpoint          The handle, which goes in front of every command this policy sends.
  CHAT_MSG_PREFIX   konfer.CHAT_MSG_PREFIX, the mod's, every endpoint sharing the one.
  protocol          konfer.protocol.
  comms             The descriptor KoreComms reads. Nothing in it says anything about what this policy sends, that not
                    being Kore's to know.
  WindowTitle(what) The window's title while this policy is active, "<Konfer> Suicide Kings - WHAT".
  (Konfer's)        SendAM, CSendAM, CSendChannelAM, CSendGroupAM, SendGuildAM, CSendGuildAM, SendWhisperAM, CSendWhisperAM,

  (Kore's)          SendText, SendGuildText, SendWhisper, SendWarning. There is no OnCommReceived: a policy never subscribes,

  Standings(cfgid, listid, uid) -> record
                    rl.standings[uid], creating the table and the record, every StandingFields name starting at 0. Nil for a

  AddEvent(cfgid, cmd, ...)
                    Section 7.
  Members(cfgid, listid) -> uids
                    rl.users, the stored order, or {}.
  UserConfigs(kuid) -> { { id, name }, ... }

                    KUID, which a rename does not change, and everything it keeps about a person is keyed by uid.

9.  State the platform keeps on the policy
------------------------------------------

Read these, never write them. A policy's own state on its own table must not use these names.

  currentid       Id of the config this policy has in hand, or nil. Written only by konfer:SetPolicyCurrent.
  cfg             That config's table, or nil. Always agrees with currentid.
  sortedlists     { { id }, ... } by sortorder then name, or nil before the Lists page has sorted them.
  sortedusers     { { id, main, hasalts, tree }, ... }, mains by name with alts folded under them. Rebuilt by the Users page.
  built           True once this policy's panels have been built (konfer:BuildPolicyUI).
  foyerinfo       The foyer frame of section 3.
  listsframe, listscfgframe, itemsframe, usersframe, adminframe, adminextraframe
                  The frames the policy's panels are built into on the Lists page's two tabs, the item editor, the Users
                  page and the Admin tab's two, nil for a policy without that panel.
  liststabs, configtabs
                  The policy's own tabs, by name, each { def, client }, and on the Config page pending.
  listscfgheight, itemsheight, adminheight
                  What the matching Construct answered.
  createuserdlg   The policy's create user dialog, made the first time Create is clicked while it is active.

There is one window and it is the mod's: konfer.mainwin, with the handful of its widgets other files reach in konfer.qf.

11. What the platform does not yet do
-------------------------------------

What a policy other than Suicide Kings will need and cannot have today.

Decided, not built
  - Nothing at present.

Undesigned, and not to be inferred
  - Nothing at present.

Which policy runs loot is the player's to choose. Becoming master looter says how things stand and offers the way to change
them (konfer:BecameMasterLooter): the foyer where the active policy has no config, which policy is set to hand out loot
where it has one among others, and nothing where it is the only policy switched on.
